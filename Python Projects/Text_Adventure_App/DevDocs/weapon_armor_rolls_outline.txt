== Stat Rolls ==

Strength = 1d6 + 8 // range 9 - 14
HP = 1d8 + 12 // range 13 - 20
Luck = 1d6 + 6 // range 7 - 12

(these rolls may be changed to account for armor values)

Strength is used to calculate if an attack hits vs the opps hit
Max Health is the maximum health available
Luck can be used in battle and when asked to Test Your Luck. To use luck, roll 2d6 and if the players
    luck score is higher than the roll, they succeed. In combat, when dealing damage or taking damage, the player can test their luck to increase or
    reduce the damage.
    Each use decreases the luck score by 1

Initial max values cannot be increased or exceeded (besides luck potion)

== Provisions ==

Begin with 10 provisions, may rest when given the chance (for now) and eat 1 meal, each meal restores 5 HP

== Potions ==

Start with 1 of 3 potions, Health/Strength/Luck

- Health Potion: restores health to initial max HP
- Strength Potion: restores strength to initial max STR
- Luck Potion: restores luck to initial max Luck + 1 to max

Starting potions can be used twice

== Combat ==

Player character/NPC combatant each roll 2d6 + the player/NPC Strength
Add any increases/decreases from weapon
Winner then deals damage

Attack success formula = 2d6 + STR + WeapSuccessBonus
Damage dealt = 1d4 + WeapDamageBonus - OppArmor

When dealing/taking damage, we can now use luck to increase/decrease the damage

Attack success ties means nobody deals damage
Minimum damage is 0
Combat ends when a character reaches 0 remaining health

== Traps ==

Some rooms/locations may have some traps and/or tests of strength or luck
Ex: a surprise pit trap would require a test of strength or luck
    strength/luck check is basically just a stat check to avoid damage

Failing the check will incure health damage and reduce the Player Character strength value by 1 or luck stat by an additional 1
Or possibly instant death other than the players health reduced to 0
Ex: the player didn't find a rope to cross a deep pit, so they try a jump and failed
